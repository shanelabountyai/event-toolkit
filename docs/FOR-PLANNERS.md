# Event Planner Productivity Suite

**Try it:** https://event-toolkit.vercel.app — click *"Load a demo event"*. No sign-up, no card,
nothing to install.

---

## The problem this solves

You run twenty to forty events a year. Every one of them lives in a different pile of files.

The brief is a doc. The budget is a spreadsheet somebody else owns. The run of show is a third
sheet you print at 6am. The badge scans arrive as a CSV with four spellings of the same company.
The post-mortem happens in the taxi to the airport, if at all.

You can work like that. Most people do. **The cost isn't the admin — it's that nothing you learn
at one event reaches the next one.**

The same venue overcharges you twice. The same AV failure happens three years running. The
freight quote that came in 40% over gets accepted again, because the person who noticed last time
has moved teams. And when your CFO asks what the $145,000 booth actually returned, you assemble
the answer by hand — differently each time, from whichever spreadsheets you can still find.

## What this does

**One event brief feeds all seven tools.** Write it once — objectives, audience, dates, venue,
budget shell, stakeholders, success metrics, risks — and everything downstream reads from it. You
never retype a date or a venue name.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Event brief** | Guided intake, then a structured brief you can export or share |
| **Promo campaign kit** | 18 assets — emails, social, landing page, sales outreach — generated from the brief |
| **Registration pacing** | Tracks signups against a target curve and tells you which asset to push |
| **Logistics pack** | Run of show, staffing, shipping, checklist, contacts, issue log — printable, and shareable to on-site staff by link |
| **Budget tracker** | Line items with budgeted / committed / actual, variance flagged as it drifts |
| **Lead triage** | Import badge scans, catch duplicates, score and route to reps, draft follow-ups |
| **ROI report** | Attribution, cost per lead, a five-dimension scorecard, year-on-year |
| **Post-mortem** | Turns your issue log, budget variances and ROI into lessons — **and puts them in front of you when you plan the next event** |

That last one is the point of the whole thing. The loop closes.

## Why you might actually use it

**It works without an account.** Open it, use it, close it. Your data stays in your browser —
no server, no login, nothing to procure. Sign in only if you want a shared workspace and your
events on more than one device. Local-only is a supported way to work, not a trial that expires.

**It doesn't overwrite your work.** Edit a generated email and hit regenerate — your edit survives,
with a "revert to generated" if you change your mind. Same everywhere: it never auto-merges a
duplicate lead it isn't sure about, never picks a winner when two people edit the same thing, never
writes a number back to your brief without asking.

**The logistics pack survives contact with a venue.** It prints properly — 11pt minimum, no row
split across a page, headers repeated, form controls printed as their values instead of empty
boxes. And you can text a read-only link to an AV tech so they can see the run of show and report a
problem, without giving them an account or access to anything else.

**It imports the files you actually have.** Badge scans, registrant exports, CRM pipeline dumps,
survey results — five different files with five different header conventions, mapped automatically.
Nothing is saved until you've seen what it read.

**Attendee data is treated as somebody else's.** The people whose badges you scanned never agreed
to anything with you. Two of the five roles cannot see lead data at all, every read is logged, and
if one of them asks what you hold — or asks you to delete it — there's a screen that finds every
record across all seven tools in one search.

## What it tells you that you might not want to hear

**Your ROI number is a choice, and it says so.** Change the attribution window from 30 days to 180
and the same data can flip the verdict from "don't repeat this" to "repeat it". The tool shows you
how far the number moves across plausible windows rather than presenting one and calling it truth.

**It won't claim to have validated anything it hasn't.** There's a page that reads your actual
data and reports what it says about the tool's own defaults — the variance thresholds, the lead
scoring, the attribution windows. Right now, after two test events, it says:

> **0 holding up · 0 worth revisiting · 9 waiting on data**

Because the honest answer, on that much data, is *we don't know yet*. It'll say the same to you
until you've run enough events for it to mean something. It also lists what it can never tell you
— like duplicates you never caught, which leave no trace.

## What it deliberately doesn't do

- **No integrations.** No HubSpot, Marketo, Cvent or Splash sync. Everything comes in by typing or
  CSV. That's a v1 boundary, not an oversight.
- **No AI writing your copy.** The generated assets are templates filled from your brief. Nothing
  is sent to a language model. If the copy is weak, the fix is a better template — and you'll
  always edit it anyway.
- **No real-time collaboration.** Two people can work the same event across devices, and conflicts
  are surfaced for you to resolve, but there are no live cursors.

## Being straight with you

This has been run end to end twice, by testers, on realistic invented events. **No real event has
gone through it yet.**

That means the defaults — when a budget line gets flagged, how leads are scored, what counts as
influenced pipeline — are considered guesses, not findings. They're labelled that way inside the
product, and the calibration page exists specifically to tell you when your own data starts
disagreeing with them.

If you want a tool that's been proven across a thousand events, this isn't it yet. If you want one
that's honest about which of its numbers are opinions, and that closes the loop from post-mortem
back into planning, it's worth twenty minutes.
